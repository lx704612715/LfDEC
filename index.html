Generalization requires separating general information from instance-specific details

To achieve generalization, we separate generalizable information from instance-specific details. Learning should focus on the general parts, leaving the details to be resolved through sensory feedback during execution. This separation shifts the burden of handling instance-specific variations from learning to execution, which we believe is a principle for achieving generalization in robot learning.

Environmental constraints enable this separation in contact-rich manipulation

We find that environmental constraints (ECs) enable this separation in contact-rich manipulation. An object’s articulation encodes its functionality and imposes constraints during interaction. Objects with the same functionality often share these constraints, while their geometric parameters vary. Learning should capture the shared constraints to transfer across object instances.

We leave instance-specific geometric details to compliant execution. Compliance automatically adapts the robot’s motion to the encountered constraints, and the resulting force–motion relationships reveal their underlying geometry. This balances general learning with adaptive execution.
